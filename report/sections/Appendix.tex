\subsection{Preprocessing Pipeline Details}\label{app:preprocessing}

This appendix provides the full mathematical formulation of the preprocessing pipeline
summarized in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsubsection{Binarization as an Auxiliary Mask}

Each image is first converted to grayscale and an Otsu-thresholded binary version is derived
from it. Otsu's method determines the optimal threshold $t^*$ by maximizing inter-class variance:
\begin{equation}
t^* = \arg\max_t \sigma_B^2(t) = \arg\max_t \omega_0(t) \omega_1(t) [\mu_0(t) - \mu_1(t)]^2
\end{equation}
where $\omega_0(t)$ and $\omega_1(t)$ are the background and foreground class probabilities,
and $\mu_0(t)$, $\mu_1(t)$ their respective means. In the resulting mask, text pixels are 0
(black) and background pixels are 255 (white). The mask is used to: (i) detect IAM guide lines,
(ii) locate left-margin black columns and top/bottom black rows, and (iii) compute tight-crop
bounding boxes. For RIMES, the coordinates derived from the mask are applied to the grayscale
image, which is what is cropped, resized, and saved. For IAM, the coordinates are applied to
the binary image, which is what is cropped, resized, and saved.

\subsubsection{Tight Cropping}
\label{app:tight-cropping}

To eliminate excessive whitespace while preserving text content, we compute tight crop
coordinates using axial projections on the binary mask. For a binary image $I$ of size
$h \times w$, we invert it ($I_{\text{inv}} = 255 - I$) and compute row and column projections:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{row}}(i) = \sum_{j=1}^{w} \mathbb{I}[I_{\text{inv}}(i,j) > 0], \quad
S_{\text{col}}(j) = \sum_{i=1}^{h} \mathbb{I}[I_{\text{inv}}(i,j) > 0]
\end{equation}
Adaptive thresholds determine content-bearing rows and columns, based on a minimum ink-density
ratio $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{row}} = \max(10, \lfloor \rho \cdot w \rfloor), \quad
\tau_{\text{col}} = \max(10, \lfloor \rho \cdot h \rfloor)
\end{equation}
We use $\rho = 0.018$ for RIMES lines and a slightly stricter $\rho = 0.02$ for IAM, to
compensate for residual guide-line artifacts left after region extraction. The bounding box is
\begin{equation}
y_1 = \min\{i : S_{\text{row}}(i) > \tau_{\text{row}}\}, \quad y_2 = \max\{i : S_{\text{row}}(i) > \tau_{\text{row}}\}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
x_1 = \min\{j : S_{\text{col}}(j) > \tau_{\text{col}}\}, \quad x_2 = \max\{j : S_{\text{col}}(j) > \tau_{\text{col}}\}
\end{equation}
If no row or no column exceeds its threshold, the image is discarded. A 3-pixel internal trim,
applied only along the row axis, removes residual guide lines:
$y_1 \leftarrow \min(y_1+3,\, y_2-1)$ and $y_2 \leftarrow \max(y_2-3,\, y_1+1)$, while
$x_1, x_2$ are left untouched. This is followed by 5-pixel padding on \emph{all four} sides,
clipped to the image bounds, to prevent text from touching borders. The resulting coordinates
$(y_1, y_2, x_1, x_2)$ are used to crop the image.

\subsubsection{Aspect-Preserving Resize to Square}

For IAM, images are normalized to $448\times448$ pixels by first centering them on a white
(255) square canvas of side $s = \max(h, w)$ with offsets
\begin{equation}
\text{offset}_y = \lfloor (s - h) / 2 \rfloor, \quad \text{offset}_x = \lfloor (s - w) / 2 \rfloor
\end{equation}
The canvas is then resized to $448\times448$ using area interpolation, preserving the original
aspect ratio without distortion.

\subsubsection{Ink-Based Quality Filtering}
\label{app:ink-ratio-filter}

We define the ink ratio of an image as the fraction of pixels with intensity below 250.
Images whose ink ratio falls below a threshold are discarded (and stored in a separate
``rejected'' folder for inspection). The thresholds are: $0.010$ for full IAM images,
$0.010$ for IAM patches, $0.015$ for RIMES $448\times448$ batches (which contain white
padding), and $0.020$ for RIMES mosaics.

\subsubsection{IAM Dataset Preprocessing}

IAM forms contain pre-printed guide lines that must be excluded. We detect horizontal lines
using morphological opening (erosion followed by dilation, 2 iterations) on the inverted binary
mask, with a kernel of width $k_w = \max(30, \lfloor w/15 \rfloor)$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Opening}(I, K) = \text{Dilate}(\text{Erode}(I, K), K)
\end{equation}
Valid lines satisfy $w_c > 0.3w$, $h_c < 20$, and area $> 100$ pixels. Detected lines are
sorted top-to-bottom and the handwritten region is selected as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three or more lines detected:} the region is extracted between the second and
third detected lines,
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{start}} = y_{\text{bottom}}^{(2)} + 8, \quad y_{\text{end}} = y_{\text{top}}^{(3)} - 8
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Exactly two lines detected:} the region is extracted between them,
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{start}} = y_{\text{bottom}}^{(1)} + 8, \quad y_{\text{end}} = y_{\text{top}}^{(2)} - 8
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Fewer than two lines detected:} a secondary detection pass estimates guide-line
positions from a smoothed row-density profile. The row-wise ink density is smoothed with a
uniform filter (window size 5), and rows exceeding the 95th percentile of the smoothed profile
are grouped into contiguous segments (gap tolerance of 5 pixels). If this pass yields three or
more segments, or exactly two, the same rules above are re-applied to the segments; otherwise
the pipeline uses the fixed fallback.
\item \textbf{Fallback:} if no reliable lines are found by either pass,
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{start}} = \lfloor 0.22h \rfloor, \quad y_{\text{end}} = \lfloor 0.85h \rfloor
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Two sanity checks guard against degenerate cases. First, if $y_{\text{start}} \geq y_{\text{end}}$
after any of the branches above, the pipeline reverts to the fixed fallback
$[0.22h, 0.85h]$. Second, $y_{\text{start}}$ and $y_{\text{end}}$ are clipped to the valid image
range, $y_{\text{start}} \leftarrow \max(0, y_{\text{start}})$ and
$y_{\text{end}} \leftarrow \min(h, y_{\text{end}})$. As a final safeguard, if the extracted region
is empty or smaller than $30 \times 50$ pixels (height $\times$ width), extraction reverts to
the fixed fallback region. Region selection is performed on the binary mask, and the same rows
are extracted from both the grayscale and binary images to keep them spatially aligned.

\textbf{Left black column removal.} Let $r_j$ be the fraction of black pixels in column $j$ of
the binary region. We remove all columns to the left of
\begin{equation}
j^* = \min\{ j : r_k \leq 0.02 \;\; \forall k \in [j,\, j+19] \}
\end{equation}
i.e., the start of the first run of at least 20 consecutive ``clean'' columns. This handles both
sharp black bands and gradual decay at scan borders. If no such run exists, no column is removed.

\textbf{Full image and patches.} After column removal, tight cropping ($\rho = 0.02$,
Appendix~\ref{app:tight-cropping}) is applied and the cropped binary region is resized to a
square $448\times448$ image (the full sample). If its ink ratio is below the threshold, the
whole form is discarded. Otherwise, the cropped region is also divided into a $2\times2$ grid
of patches (the last row/column absorbing any remainder); each patch is independently centered
on a square canvas, resized to $448\times448$, and kept only if it passes the ink-ratio filter.
Each form therefore yields up to five images. Figure~\ref{fig:iam-preprocessing} illustrates
the pipeline.

\subsubsection{RIMES Dataset Preprocessing}
\label{app:rimes-preprocessing}

RIMES consists of individual text lines. Lines are grouped by writer, identified by the split name
and the writer index in the filename (e.g., \texttt{eval2011-0}). The goal is to turn the lines of
each writer into $448\times448$ images with a text density comparable to the IAM samples. Two
constants define the layout: each image contains $N_{\text{batch}} = 5$ rows of text, so every row
has height $H = \lfloor 448 / N_{\text{batch}} \rfloor = 89$ pixels. The processing has three
stages: (i) each line is cleaned and resized independently, (ii) the lines of a writer are
assembled into $448\times448$ batches, and (iii) batches are combined into mosaics.

\paragraph{Stage 1: line-level processing.}
Each line is converted to grayscale, and an auxiliary Otsu mask is derived from it. The mask is
used only to compute crop coordinates, which are then applied to the grayscale line; only the
grayscale line is resized and saved. Each line undergoes the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Vertical trim:} dark bands at the top and bottom of the scan are removed. We
discard the rows in which more than $50\%$ of the pixels are black, keeping the range between the
first and the last row that are not.
\item \textbf{Tight cropping:} the whitespace around the text is removed using the
projection-based algorithm with $\rho = 0.018$ (Appendix~\ref{app:tight-cropping}).
\item \textbf{Splitting:} lines wider than $896$ pixels are split at the midpoint into two
segments, to avoid excessively long lines after resizing.
\item \textbf{Height normalization:} every line is resized to height $H = 89$ pixels, preserving
the aspect ratio, so that its width becomes $w' = \text{round}(w \cdot H / h)$. No horizontal
padding is applied.
\end{enumerate}
After this stage, every line has the same height and a variable width.

\paragraph{Stage 2: batch construction (per writer).}
The lines of a writer are concatenated horizontally into a single long strip, with a $15$-pixel
white gap between consecutive lines so that they are not read as one. The strip is then cut every
$448$ pixels into slices of size $89\times448$ (the last one is right-padded with white). Note
that a slice does not correspond to an original line: it may contain the end of one line and the
beginning of the next. Finally, $N_{\text{batch}} = 5$ consecutive slices are stacked vertically,
giving an image of size $445\times448$, and $3$ white rows are appended at the bottom to obtain a
$448\times448$ batch. Batches with too little ink (typically the last one, which is often almost
empty) are discarded according to the ink-ratio filter (See Appendix~\ref{app:ink-ratio-filter}).

\paragraph{Stage 3: mosaics.}
To obtain more samples per writer, the surviving batches are combined into $2\times2$ mosaics:
each batch is downscaled to $224\times224$ and four batches, taken in generation order, are tiled
into a $448\times448$ image. For example, a writer with $9$ batches yields three mosaics: batches
$1$--$4$, batches $5$--$8$, and a final one made of batch $9$ plus three batches already used, with
the tile positions randomly shuffled. If a writer has fewer than four batches, the missing tiles
are left white. Mosaics with too little ink are discarded according to the ink-ratio filter 
(See Appendix~\ref{app:ink-ratio-filter}). Both batches and mosaics are saved as
outputs. Figure~\ref{fig:rimes-preprocessing} illustrates stages 1 and 2.

\centering

\caption{Preprocessing of RIMES text lines for a given writer. Each original line (left, e.g. \texttt{eval2011-0\_000001.jpg}) is binarized via Otsu thresholding and tight-cropped (blue bounding boxes, center) using the projection-based algorithm; the corresponding grayscale crop is then extracted and resized to a common height (right of center). \subsection{Training Dynamics}\label{app:training-curves}

Figure~\ref{fig:training_curves} reports training and validation loss curves for the ResNet-18 
backbone across all 12 loss$\times$split combinations. Across nearly all configurations, 
training loss decreases monotonically, consistent with stable optimization; however, 
validation-loss behavior differs substantially by loss function and split. Under BCE, 
validation loss tracks training loss relatively closely on the same-dataset splits 
(IAM$\to$IAM, RIMES$\to$RIMES), while the cross-dataset splits show an early divergence between 
the two curves (e.g., IAM$\to$RIMES), consistent with the same-vs-cross-dataset generalization 
gap discussed in Section~\ref{sec:cross-dataset}. Under Contrastive and, more markedly, 
under Triplet Loss, validation loss is considerably noisier and frequently non-monotonic, 
with pronounced oscillations and, in several cases (e.g., Triplet on IAM$\to$IAM and IAM$\to$RIMES), 
no clear downward trend despite steadily decreasing training loss -- indicative of a widening 
train--validation gap rather than smooth joint convergence. Training run lengths also vary 
considerably across configurations, from as few as 6 epochs (Triplet, RIMES$\to$RIMES) to 
over 40 (BCE, IAM$\to$IAM), reflecting the early-stopping criterion (patience 5, Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams}) 
being triggered at different points depending on the stability of the validation signal for each configuration.
